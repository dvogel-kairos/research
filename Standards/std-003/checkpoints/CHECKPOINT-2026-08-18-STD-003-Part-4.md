## Positions taken that a reviewer may want to overturn

The triad of concept, designation and representation, held as three separately versionable artifacts. The concept is the identity; a designation may never be a key. Grounded in ISO 704's object, concept, definition, designation chain and ISO/IEC 11179-3's four common facilities.

A closed set of nine change kinds, of which `CONCEPT_REPLACEMENT` exists in order to be refused. A change of meaning is never a version of the same thing, and the remedy is a new concept identity that forces every dependent to rebind visibly.

A declared extension effect, checked for consistency against the declared kinds, with `INCOMPARABLE` treated as a concept replacement unless justified.

Retrospectivity as a declared property of a change, which determines whether figures already published are now wrong.

The classification test set as the bridge: borderline instances with asserted membership, run against a version pair, disproving a claim of no change and never proving one. The same asymmetric pattern `Part 2` uses for its worked examples.

Unapproved definition versions are admissible, marked in every read, and countable. The alternative guarantees the registry lags the estate.

Ten closed lineage edge kinds including `FILTER_DEPENDENCY` and `DEFAULT_SUBSTITUTION`, the two nobody records. Grain recorded on every node and edge. Assertion method and tool version pinned on every edge.

Impact is not reachability. An impact set spans lineage, realisation, concept relations, dependency registrations and model interfaces, and `IMPACT_LINEAGE_ONLY` exists so that a data flow traversal cannot be passed off as an assessment.

Seven consequence classes, of which `SEMANTIC_DRIFT` and `HISTORICAL_RECLASSIFICATION` are the two nothing else surfaces. Materiality is never computed; it defaults to `IMPACT_UNASSESSED` and requires a named actor.

Inferential models are registered by identity, version and interface only, with training definition drift detectable. Their behaviour is `Part 13`'s.

## Research findings

**ISO/IEC 11179 was restructured for its 2023 editions and the part numbering changed.** Parts 1 and 3 are edition 4 of January 2023; Parts 31, 32, 33 and 35 are new. A citation to a part number without an edition is likely to name a withdrawn document. Part 3 also has an Amendment 1 dated 2026 whose content was not obtained.

**ISO/IEC 11179-5:2015 is under revision**, expected to be replaced by a DIS.

**The status of ISO/IEC 11179-4:2004 within the restructured series could not be established.** This matters: section 3.6's definition quality clauses rest on it, and if it was withdrawn those clauses have no standing source.

**ISO 704:2022** is the fourth edition, July 2022, cancelling the 2009 edition.

**ISO/IEC 19763-1:2023** supersedes the 2015 edition and states explicitly that the series specifies no physical registry structure, which is why this part specifies entities and obligations rather than a schema.

**Neither ISO/IEC 11179 nor ISO/IEC 19763 specifies lineage or impact analysis at all.** Two of the three subjects the brief named for this part are almost entirely unsourced, and section 10.8 lists twelve unsourced requirements accordingly. This is the most important finding in section 10.

No expression of an information model is specified, deliberately, following ISO/IEC 19763-1's own silence.
